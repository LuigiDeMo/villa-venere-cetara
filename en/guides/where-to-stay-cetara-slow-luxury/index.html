Living Cetara

Staying in Cetara: a slower base for exploring the Amalfi Coast

Choosing Cetara does not mean giving up Amalfi, Positano or Ravello. It means visiting them by day, then returning to a lived-in fishing village, its harbour and food, and a private home above the sea.

The short answer

Cetara is particularly well suited to travellers who want a less frantic stay, enjoy travelling by sea and prefer to separate day trips from genuine holiday time. Seasonal ferries connect the village with other coastal towns, while buses provide the land alternative. A private driver can simplify more complex days such as Ravello or Pompeii.

  • Cetara is a place to stay, not merely a stop
  • Amalfi and Positano can become day trips by sea
  • Ravello and the Vesuvian sites need different planning
  • Alternating full days with slow days makes the trip more enjoyable

Why Cetara works as a base

Cetara retains the scale of a fishing village: the harbour, beaches, restaurants and food shops belong to daily life and are reached on foot. You can begin the morning without immediately getting into a car and have dinner in the village after an excursion.

The advantage is not being at the geometric centre of every attraction. It is having somewhere worth staying. An unplanned morning, a swim or a lunch built around local anchovies are not gaps in the itinerary; they are part of experiencing the coast.

Iconic places as day trips

Amalfi and Positano suit days by sea whenever seasonal services and conditions allow. Ravello always requires a road section, either by bus through the Amalfi area or with a private driver. Pompeii, Herculaneum and Mount Vesuvius deserve an early start and a dedicated day.

The most useful rule is not to compress too many destinations into one day. One main visit, an unhurried lunch and a return with breathing room usually create better memories than four rushed stops.

  • By sea: Amalfi, Positano and private cruises
  • By road: Ravello, Villa Rufolo and Villa Cimbrone
  • History and landscape: Pompeii, Herculaneum and Vesuvius
  • A local day: Cetara, food, terrace and sea

A realistic rhythm for your stay

For three nights, Cetara plus one classic coastal day is sensible. Four or five nights allow time for Ravello or Pompeii as well. On longer stays, alternate each substantial excursion with a lighter day between the villa, village and sea.

Families, older travellers and larger groups should treat steps, heat, luggage and waiting time as part of the itinerary rather than details to solve at the last minute.

Choosing between ferry, bus and driver

The ferry offers views and removes the stress of the coastal road, but it is seasonal and depends on sea conditions. Buses serve more places and provide a useful alternative, though they can be busy. A private driver costs more but enables early departures, agreed stops and a more flexible return.

The best choice depends on the day rather than a universal rule. Check official services before leaving and keep a workable alternative.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cetara convenient for exploring the Amalfi Coast?

Yes, particularly for travellers who want to use seasonal ferries and plan Ravello and the archaeological sites as dedicated days.

Do I need a car?

No. The village is walkable and ferries, buses, trains and transfers can be combined. A car can be useful, but traffic and parking require care.

How many nights should I stay?

Three nights offer an introduction; four or five make it easier to alternate excursions with slower time in Cetara.
